Clear space + minimum size

Reserve clear space equal to the height of the icon around all edges of the mark. Don't crowd it. Minimum display size: 24dp at 1x on digital, 12mm on print. Below those sizes, use the icon-only mark.

Don'ts

Don't

Recolor the marks. The teal and orange are the brand. Other palettes are forbidden in logo contexts.

Don't

Outline, stroke, drop-shadow, gradient, or otherwise stylize the mark. The mark ships as-is.

Don't

Stretch, skew, rotate beyond horizontal, or otherwise distort the mark's proportions.

Don't

Place the mark on a low-contrast background where the wordmark falls below WCAG AA against the surface behind it.

04, Identity

Iconography.

Material Symbols Rounded leads. The MD3 Expressive rounded silhouette is the canonical When icon style, warmer than outlined, more confident than sharp. Variable-font across four axes, FILL, weight, grade, optical size. Selection state fills the icon (FILL 1); everything else stays at FILL 0. Weight follows the label-weight of the surrounding text.

Fallback: if a needed glyph isn't present in Material Symbols Rounded (rare; the set is comprehensive), reach for a same-shape, same-spec rounded icon (Material Icons Rounded, or a custom-drawn rounded SVG at 24dp grid + 2dp stroke). Never mix in an outlined or sharp icon, the silhouette has to stay rounded across every surface.

05, Expression

Imagery.

When the image carries the brand more than the words, photography direction matters. Until a curated photo set lands in _inbox/photography/, this section is a direction guide, what to shoot, what to commission, what to avoid.

Photography direction

Do

  • Real people in real moments. A parent at a kitchen table reading a benefits letter. A new hire's first week. A retirement send-off.
  • Natural light. Soft, late-afternoon, window-side. Never studio-flat.
  • Warm neutral wardrobes. Mirrors the Linen palette, off-white, sand, muted teal, soft orange accents.
  • Wide and quiet. Subject takes the right third, room to breathe on the left for headline overlay.
  • Diverse, age-inclusive. Our workforce includes 22-year-olds taking COBRA seriously for the first time and 64-year-olds approaching Medicare.

Don't

  • Stock-photo "team huddle around laptop" compositions.
  • Posed handshakes in conference rooms. Already a B2B SaaS cliché.
  • Doctors in white coats with stethoscopes. We're not a clinical brand.
  • Synthetic AI imagery with extra fingers, melted hands, uncanny smiles. If the budget is tight, illustration is honest; AI mid-journey is not.
  • HDR-saturated or overly stylized color grading. Linen surfaces deserve restraint.

Drop reference photos or a moodboard PDF into _inbox/photography/ and this section renders them. Until then, an illustration system, also pending, would be the second-best fallback.

06, Expression Live

Motion.

Calm but alive. Springs where things grow or shrink. Emphasized curves where things slide in or out. No bounce on press, that reads as jitter. The full token reference + live demos live on the Design page; this section is the principle behind the choices.

Principles

Spring spatial = "alive"

A small overshoot signals that the element has its own physics, not that it's a placeholder waiting for instructions. Reserve for layout transitions, sheet open/close, the tab strip pill-to-circle transition.

Spring effects = "calm"

No overshoot. For color, opacity, blur, hint pulse. The transition is felt, not seen.

Emphasized = "snappy"

For press feedback, state-layer fade, shell motion (subnav, scrim). Fast in, slow out, no overshoot.

Reduced-motion always wins

Every animation is wrapped in @media (prefers-reduced-motion: reduce). The site is still readable without movement.

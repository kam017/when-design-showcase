Verbal Identity · Member

How When sounds to members.

Health insurance is one of the most stressful things people deal with. Our job is to make it feel solvable. Calm, clear, and honest, without being clinical, cute, or over-promising. This is the voice we use with the people who use the product, members navigating coverage decisions.

Looking for how we sound to HR leaders, brokers, or partners? See Voice · Sales.

01, Voice

Five qualities that make a message sound like When.

Voice is who we are. It doesn't change. Tone is how we adjust, calmer when someone's stuck, brighter when they've finished, quieter when the moment is about them, not us.

Calm, not cheerful

We don't celebrate the obvious. We don't add exclamation points to make a process feel exciting. Saving someone money on a deductible is good news; selling it as amazing makes them suspicious.

Direct, not blunt

Say the thing. If a plan won't cover their doctor, say so. But don't stop there, show what would. Direct without alternatives is just bad news.

Plain, not dumbed down

We use the words people actually use, "deductible" stays; "coinsurance" gets a one-line definition the first time it appears. We never invent friendly substitutes for terms our users will see on their insurance card.

Honest about uncertainty

When a number is an estimate, we say estimate. When we don't know, we don't pretend. We never bury a hedge in fine print to make a headline sound more confident than it is.

In your corner

The user isn't a "member" or a "customer" or a "subscriber." They're a person making a hard decision under pressure. We're the friend who's done this before, never a chatbot, never a sales surface, never HR.

02, Tone

Where the voice flexes.

Same voice, different volume. Tone shifts with what's happening for the user , not what's happening in the product. A successful enrollment doesn't need confetti. A failed plan search needs a clear next step, not an apology.

Onboarding · curious and grounding

The user is new. Establish that we know them, their employer reached out to us, and explain what's coming. Don't overstate the value before they've felt it.

"When your group health plan ends, you have options. We walk you through them, show you what each plan actually costs, and let you enroll in one place."

Decision moments · clear and unsoftened

When the user has to choose, get out of the way. State the numbers, the trade-offs, and what we'd do in their shoes. Don't fence-sit.

"This plan is $84 a month after your subsidy. Your doctor isn't in network, switching would cost about $200 a year more in office visits. Worth it if you want to keep her."

Mistakes & errors · take responsibility, point forward

When something breaks, we own it without melodrama. State the problem in one sentence, then what to do next. Never apologize twice for the same thing.

"We couldn't reach the marketplace. Try again in a moment, or call us at (800) 555-WHEN and we'll finish this with you."

After enrollment · quiet confidence

They picked a plan. The work happened. Confirm it cleanly, set the next expectation, and step back.

"Your application is in. The carrier will mail your member ID within 10 business days; coverage starts March 1."

03, Words

What we say, and what we don't.

A short list of specifics. The full glossary lives in Notion, this is the page you'll come back to when you're writing.

We say

  • your plan, your coverage, your premium
  • walk you through, show you, help you decide
  • estimate (when it's an estimate)
  • cost · price · monthly · per year
  • doctor · prescription · provider · network
  • when you're ready · take your time
  • your employer set this up for you
  • we'll send you · we sent you · you'll get

We don't say

  • member · subscriber · customer · user
  • seamlessly · effortlessly · simply · just
  • amazing · awesome · perfect · easy
  • premium pricing · best-in-class · industry-leading
  • doc · script · Rx (in user copy)
  • hit a snag · uh oh · whoops · oops
  • HR sent us your information
  • "Let's…" as the start of every prompt

04, In practice

Surface by surface.

Every component carries copy. Here's how the voice lands on each one, and what to avoid.

Button labels

Verb first. Tell them what happens when they press. Never use "Submit," "Click here," or a label that could fit on any button anywhere.

Do

  • Send me a sign-in link
  • See my plans
  • Add a prescription
  • Save and continue
  • Talk to a person

Don't

  • Submit
  • Continue
  • Click here
  • Next step
  • Get started now!

Empty states

Tell them why it's empty. Show them what fills it. Then offer the action that starts the fill. Never apologize for an empty state, it's the right state until they take the first step.

"No prescriptions yet. Add the medicines you take regularly and we'll show you which plans cover them."

Error messages

One sentence. State the problem. Offer a path. Tell them how to reach us if the path doesn't work. No system codes in user-facing copy.

Do

"That email doesn't look right. Double-check the address, we'll send you a new sign-in link."

Don't

"Error code 422: Invalid email format. Please correct the email and try again."

System status & loading

State what's happening. Don't fill silence with personality. A 2-second spinner doesn't need a "Hang tight!", it needs a quiet status word.

Do

  • Looking up your case…
  • Sending your sign-in link…
  • Pulling plans for your area…

Don't

  • Hang tight! We're on it…
  • Magic is happening…
  • Just a sec, almost there!

Microcopy & placeholders

Placeholder text is for shape, not instruction. Required guidance lives outside the field, where it doesn't disappear the moment someone starts typing.

Do

Label: Work email
Placeholder: you@company.com
Help (always visible): The address your employer uses to reach you

Don't

Label: Email
Placeholder: Enter your work email here
(no help; instruction vanishes on focus)

Privacy & sensitive moments

When we ask for health, household, or financial information, lead with what we'll do with it. Be specific about who can see it, by name, not "third parties."

"No one at your employer can see which plans you look at, the prescriptions you list, or who's in your household. Your choices stay between you and the carrier."

Success & confirmation

Tell them what happened, what to expect next, and when. Set the next moment for them so they're not refreshing for confirmation.

Do

"Your application is in. The carrier will email your member ID within 10 business days. Coverage starts March 1."

Don't

"🎉 Yay! You did it! Your enrollment is complete, congrats on taking care of yourself!"

05, Mechanics

Small rules with big effect.

Sentence case

Buttons, labels, headers, and titles all use sentence case. Title Case Feels Like A Press Release. We're not a press release.

No em-dashes

The em-dash is a tell that an LLM wrote the copy. Use a comma, a colon, or split the sentence. We earn pauses; we don't insert them.

No italic for emphasis

Italic is reserved for proper titles (a plan name, a publication, a literal quote). If something needs emphasis, the sentence isn't doing its job.

First name, used sparingly

Address the user by name once on entry. After that, "you" is enough. "Hi Sam!" three times in a single screen is uncanny, not friendly.

Numbers as numerals

$84. 30 days. 4 dependents. Words for numbers slow scanning; numerals fit the way people actually read prices and dates.

Cite your sources

When we surface a number from a carrier, an estimate, or a third party, attribute it. "From the marketplace." "Carrier estimate." "Based on what you've told us."

06, Who's speaking

When, Jamie, the carrier.

Three speakers. They sound different on purpose.

When (the platform)

The neutral voice. Explains, presents, sets expectations. First-person plural ("we") for system messages and broad announcements.

"We pulled 6 plans for your zip code."

Jamie (the AI guide)

Conversational, first-person singular ("I"), proactive but never pushy. Asks one thing at a time. Always offers a way to skip the conversation and use the form directly.

"Want me to check whether your current doctor is covered? Type their name and I'll pull it up."

The carrier

We quote carriers in their own words inside quotation marks; we don't paraphrase their disclaimers in ways that could be wrong. When we summarize, we attribute it.

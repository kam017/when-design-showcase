Verbal Identity · Sales

How When sounds to buyers.

Same brand. Different audience. To members we're a calm guide; to HR leaders, brokers, and consultants we're a confident operator with the receipts. Plainspoken, outcome-led, evidence-based. Never hype.

Looking for how we sound to members? See Voice · Member.

01, Voice

Five qualities that make a sales message sound like When.

Sales voice keeps the same DNA as member voice, just turns up confidence and specificity. We never become the company we're trying to replace. No synergy. No best-in-class. No revolutionary.

Confident, not boastful

We say what we do and we cite the proof. We don't claim to be the best. We don't compare ourselves to legacy vendors by name unless the buyer asks. The work speaks; we just point at it.

Specific, not vague

Names, numbers, dates, source citations. "37% lower COBRA cost in the first quarter at NWA, sourced from Mercer Q1 2026" beats "industry-leading savings" every time.

Outcome-led, not feature-led

Lead with what the buyer's day looks like after they pick us, not what the product technically does. "Your HR team stops fielding COBRA questions" sells better than "AI-powered benefits navigator."

Honest about tradeoffs

ACA isn't always cheaper than COBRA. We say so. Our platform isn't right for every employer. We say so. Buyers trust honesty more than they trust polish, and they remember when we're the ones who told them.

Buyer-savvy

We know what a CHRO is measured on (retention, cost, compliance), what a benefits consultant is selling (advice + access), what a broker renewal looks like in October. We meet them where their year actually starts, not where ours does.

02, Tone

Where the voice flexes by surface.

Same voice, different volume. Cold outbound is short and earned. Demos are direct. Renewal moments are quiet. The shift is always in priority, never in posture.

First touch · earned, not demanding

A buyer's inbox is full. Our opening sentence has to do work: name a number they already know is broken, then offer something useful even if they never reply. No pleasantries, no "I hope this finds you well."

"Northwind reduced COBRA enrollment by 31% in Q1 by routing separations to ACA where it was cheaper. Happy to share the playbook either way, no demo required."

Proof · vendor-neutral, source-cited

When we cite results, we say where the number came from and whose method generated it. Self-reported numbers stay self-reported; we don't dress them up. If a stat is internal, the slide says "internal When data, n=12,400."

"Member NPS 71 (Q1 2026, n=8,300, methodology: post-enrollment survey at 14 days)."

Demo · direct, no hedging

In a live demo, we don't soften. We show the thing, name the value, move on. No "this is just" or "kind of." If something isn't ready, we say so and what's on the way.

"This is the enrollment screen. Average completion is 4 minutes. The two-week version of this will also pre-fill from your HRIS, that ships in June."

Renewal · gratitude without sycophancy

Renewals are about delivering on what we said. The tone is matter-of-fact pride in the work, plus an honest read of what didn't land. We never use a renewal as a sales surface.

"Year one: 41% adoption, $1.8M in employer subsidy spend recovered, two carrier escalations resolved at our end. Year two should land us at 60% adoption if we add the SMS pathway. Here's what didn't go well."

03, Words

What we say, and what we don't.

Sales pages and decks accumulate jargon if no one weeds. This is the weed list. The full glossary lives in Notion; this page is the one to re-read before sending the deck.

We say

  • cost containment · cost recovery · subsidy capture
  • employees, members, your team
  • brokers, consultants, advisors, partners
  • deliver, ship, route, enroll, recover
  • numbers, evidence, the data, what we measured
  • where we partner, how we partner
  • case file, outcome, result
  • your HR team, your benefits team, your enrollment team
  • open enrollment, separation, qualifying event
  • COBRA alternative, ACA pathway, marketplace plan
  • renewal · year over year · YoY
  • self-funded, fully-insured, ICHRA
  • "sourced from"
  • verbs first, numbers up front, sources cited

We don't say

  • synergy · synergies · synergistic anything
  • leverage (as a verb)
  • best-in-class · industry-leading · world-class
  • revolutionary · game-changer · disruption · disrupt
  • 10x · next-gen · cutting-edge
  • seamlessly · effortless · simply · just
  • unlock · empower · elevate · transform
  • "the platform" (be specific: enrollment surface, broker console)
  • "the solution" (be specific)
  • "engagement" (use: adoption, completion, NPS)
  • "holistic" · "end-to-end" · "turnkey"
  • "reach out" · "circle back" · "touch base"
  • "I hope this finds you well"
  • "we're excited to" · "thrilled to share" · "delighted"

04, In practice

Surface by surface.

Every channel carries copy. Here's how the voice lands on each one, with the patterns that have worked and the ones we keep killing in review.

Subject lines · the 60-character contract

Six words or fewer. Lowercase. State the value, not the company. The buyer's inbox renders the first 60 characters; spend them.

Do

  • cobra to ACA, the Q1 numbers
  • where your separation flow leaks
  • 4 minutes to enroll, the playbook
  • 30% lower spend at northwind, how
  • your november OE, our 60-day setup

Don't

  • Introduction: When + [Company]
  • Touching base on benefits modernization
  • Quick question about your HR strategy
  • The future of employee benefits is here
  • Game-changing AI for HR teams

Cold outbound · 3 sentences

Number on a peer. Specific thing we did. Offer that gives value even if the meeting never happens. Always include a way out.

"Northwind cut COBRA spend 31% last quarter by routing separations through ACA where it was cheaper. We built the playbook around their actual case load, not a hypothetical. Happy to share it as a PDF, no meeting needed, just reply with 'send it.' If this isn't your area, let me know who to ask."

Pitch decks · headlines that survive a screenshot

Every slide title is a sentence the buyer could screenshot and forward to their CFO. Verbs first, numbers next, source cited at the bottom. One claim per slide.

Do

  • "Your COBRA spend drops 30% when ACA is the default offer."
  • "Average member enrolls in 4 minutes, no broker call."
  • "Your benefits team gets back ~12 hours a month."

Don't

  • "Our Approach to Benefits Innovation"
  • "A New Era for Health Coverage"
  • "Driving Engagement and Adoption"

Case studies · the customer talks, we attribute

Real quote, real number, real name (when permitted). We never paraphrase outcomes. If the customer didn't say it, we don't put it in quotes. Methodology footnote on every metric.

"We sent the November email to 2,200 separating employees and 78% of them enrolled in something other than COBRA. That hadn't happened before." Lisa Chen, VP Benefits, Northwind. Internal Northwind separation logs, Q4 2025.

Conference one-liners · what they remember after the badge

Conference voice is a half-step warmer than written outbound. Still specific, still no jargon. The line they remember after a 7-second conversation is the one we lead with.

Do

  • "We route separations to ACA when it's cheaper. Last year we recovered $40M in employer subsidy spend."
  • "Most COBRA decisions are made on Day 2. We get there on Day 0."

Don't

  • "We're a holistic benefits navigation platform."
  • "Tell me about your HR transformation strategy."

LinkedIn / social · short, specific, sourced

A post is a deck slide that hit the road. Lead with the number, support with two sentences of context, end with a source citation or a real link, not a "DM me" close.

"31% of Northwind's separating employees moved to ACA instead of COBRA last quarter. The trigger was a single email sent two days after their separation date, before the COBRA election letter arrived. Source: Northwind internal data, Q1 2026 (shared with permission). The email template is on our docs site if it's useful for your team."

Sales follow-ups · respect the silence

Three follow-ups, then we stop. Each one adds something the prior didn't, not a re-ask. The last one offers a graceful exit, not a guilt trip.

Do (3rd follow-up)

"Last note from me, this isn't a sales loop. If COBRA-to-ACA routing isn't your priority this quarter, that's a useful signal. The Northwind case study is here in case it helps you make the case internally later: [link]. Wish you a good Q2."

Don't

"Just wanted to bump this back to the top of your inbox! Any chance you had a moment to think about it? Let me know if there's someone else I should connect with!"

Pricing conversations · honest math, no anchoring tricks

Per-employee-per-month, on a year contract, with the exact services listed. No ranges, no "starting at," no buried minimums. If the buyer can't reproduce the number on the back of a napkin, the pricing page is doing them dirty.

"$3.50 PEPM, 12-month, includes enrollment, AI guide, and broker console. Optional concierge add-on is $0.75 PEPM. 1,500-employee floor, prorated below that. We invoice quarterly, net 30."

05, Mechanics

Small rules, big effect.

Quantify everything

Every claim has a number. Every number has a source. "Reduces COBRA costs" becomes "reduced COBRA costs 31% at Northwind, source: internal separation logs Q1 2026." Vague claims die in review.

Verbs first

Headlines start with an action verb that the buyer's team will actually perform. "Route," "recover," "enroll," "compare." Never "Innovate," "Transform," "Empower."

Sentence case for everything

Including subject lines. Including pitch deck headlines. Title Case Reads Like A Press Release. We are not a press release.

No em-dashes

The em-dash is a tell that someone (or some model) wrote the copy in a rush. Use a comma, a colon, or a period. We earn pauses.

Cite the source inline

Stats live with their sources, not in a footnote sized 8pt. "31% (source: Northwind internal Q1 2026)" reads honest. The footnote version reads evasive.

Buyer titles, spelled out

"Chief Human Resources Officer," not "CHRO," on first reference. Same for "VP Total Rewards," "Director of Benefits," "Senior Benefits Consultant." The acronym goes in parens on first use, then becomes the shorthand. People read their own title with their full name.

Numerals for money, time, percent

$3.50 PEPM. 4 minutes. 31%. Never "four minutes" or "thirty-one percent." Numerals scan faster and signal precision.

Dates with a year

"Q1 2026," not "last quarter." Dates without years rot fast on a website. The case study cited in 2027 should still parse.

06, Who's speaking

When (the company), the customer, the exec.

Three speakers across sales surfaces. They sound different on purpose, and they're attributed differently.

When (the company)

The neutral voice on the website, the deck, the outbound. First-person plural ("we") for system-level claims. Confident, sourced, plainspoken. Never "I" unless explicitly first-person from a named individual.

"We route separations to ACA when it's cheaper. Last year that recovered $40M for employers."

The customer (in case studies + quotes)

First-person, attributed by name and title, with explicit permission. We never paraphrase customer outcomes; if it's in quotes, they said it. Their voice may not match ours, and that's the point.

"Our team stopped getting flooded with COBRA questions in November. That alone made the budget conversation easier." Lisa Chen, VP Benefits, Northwind.

The exec (PR, interviews, executive bylines)

First-person, named, with point of view. Execs are allowed to be opinionated where When-the-company stays neutral. Bylined pieces argue for things; the website describes things.

"COBRA was designed in 1985 for a workforce that doesn't exist anymore. The market has had 40 years to do something about it. We're doing the thing." Andy Hamilton, Founder & CEO, When.

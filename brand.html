Brand Foundation

When you say goodbye, we say hello.

When is the AI workforce transitions platform that helps employers reduce healthcare costs and protect their brand during employee life events. This page is the foundation, who we are, who we serve, how we sound. Everything else in the system is built on top.

01, Foundation

Who we are.

We exist because the gap between COBRA-era infrastructure and modern workforce reality has become a financial and reputational liability for employers, and a source of real stress for departing employees. Our verbal identity reflects this dual mandate: business case with confidence and specificity, human case with warmth and directness. We never sacrifice one for the other.

Our voice in one line

A knowledgeable ally who sees the full picture, the financial exposure, the compliance headaches, the human cost, and speaks to all of it without sugarcoating or fear-mongering.

Five voice attributes

The same voice in every room. Tone shifts; voice doesn't.

Direct

We lead with the point. Short sentences. Active verbs. We name the problem before we offer the solution. Not blunt or aggressive, we're not trying to scare anyone into buying.

Warm

We genuinely care about the people going through transitions. We acknowledge that these moments are hard. We treat readers as smart professionals doing their best. Not sentimental or soft, we're not a greeting card.

Grounded

We use specific numbers, real scenarios, and concrete outcomes. When we make a claim, we back it up. We speak from experience, not theory. Not academic or data-heavy, data serves the story.

Confident

We know this space. We don't hedge every statement. We use "do" instead of "can help to potentially." We take positions. Not arrogant or dismissive, we never talk down to the reader.

Modern

We sound like a company built in 2024, not 1986. Our language is current, clear, free of legacy insurance jargon unless we're deliberately defining it. Not trendy or startup-bro, no "disrupt," no "10x," no forced casualness.

02, Foundation

Who we're talking to.

Everything we write passes a simple test: would an executive at a 10,000-person self-funded employer find this useful, specific, and respectful of their time? Our primary audiences are HR and benefits leaders at self-funded employers (5,000 to 30,000 employees) plus channel partners who serve them.

HR + benefits leaders

Self-funded employers, 5,000 to 30,000 employees

Measured on cost containment, employee experience, compliance, and time saved. They've been pitched by dozens of vendors who sound the same. They already know COBRA is a problem, what they need is someone who understands the nuances.

Benefits TPAs

Third-party administrators serving self-funded clients

Care about client retention and service differentiation. We're the modern add-on that makes their book stickier, the answer when their CFO asks why claims spend is up.

Brokers + consultants

Account-driven advisors

Care about winning and keeping accounts. We're the differentiator they bring to RFPs, the proof point that lands during renewal season.

Health plans, PEOs, carriers

Service providers in the workforce-transitions stack

Complementary partners. Adverse selection is a shared problem; we route members to coverage that fits, which improves their risk pool, too.

Departing employees

Secondary audience, end users of the platform

The people going through the transition. Most of our content speaks to buyers; the platform speaks to members. See Voice · Member for how we sound to them.

What our readers have in common

  • They are busy. They skim. They need the point fast.
  • They've been pitched by dozens of vendors who all sound the same.
  • They already know COBRA is a problem; they need to see that we understand the nuances.
  • They are well-intentioned leaders who haven't had time to rethink a broken system. We don't blame them, we give them a better path.
  • Channel partners care about differentiation, client retention, and revenue. Speak to those outcomes.

04, Foundation

Naming.

The shape of the name does work. "When" sets the temporal frame, the moment of transition. Sentence case everywhere, no italics, no all-caps except in micro-labels.

When (the company)

Capital W, lowercase h-e-n. Always treated as a singular noun ("When is", not "When are"). Never abbreviated. Never possessive without the apostrophe ("When's mission"). The wordmark is the logotype; the icon is the orange "W" curl.

Workforce transitions

Our category. Broader than "offboarding," more specific than "life events." The platform is the "AI workforce transitions platform"; the category we built is "workforce transitions."

Departing employees

Human and neutral. Works for layoffs, resignations, and retirements. Never "exit-event candidates" or "transitioning workforce." Plain words for plain people.

COBRA alternative

Clear positioning. We're the modern option alongside COBRA, not a replacement for it. "COBRA alternative" beats "COBRA replacement" because the former is honest about what we are.

Jamie

The AI guide inside the product. Speaks in first-person ("I"), one question at a time, always offers a way to skip back to forms. See Voice · Member for the full Jamie spec.

05, Messaging

Architecture.

One positioning statement, one set of value props, tuned per audience. The master message stays the same; the proof points shift.

Positioning statement

When is the AI workforce transitions platform that helps employers reduce healthcare costs and protect their brand during employee life events.

Value props by audience

For HR + benefits leaders

  • Lower high-cost medical claims (up to 80% COBRA-related reduction).
  • Reduce COBRA liability and adverse selection exposure.
  • Protect the brand at exit, an exceptional experience for when it matters most.
  • Free up the benefits team, fewer COBRA questions, more strategic work.

For benefits TPAs

  • Differentiate your service tier with a modern workforce-transitions add-on.
  • Retain clients by closing the COBRA-era infrastructure gap.
  • Reduce inbound questions and admin burden during open enrollment + RIF cycles.

For brokers + consultants

  • A differentiator to bring to RFPs and renewal conversations.
  • A proof point you can quantify on a slide.
  • An expansion path with existing self-funded accounts.

For health plans, PEOs, carriers

  • Route departing members to coverage that fits their household.
  • Improve risk pool by reducing adverse selection in the COBRA channel.
  • Co-brand a workforce-transitions track inside your service catalog.

Tone spectrum

The same voice in different rooms. Voice stays consistent, tone shifts with the context.

Website

Confident, clean, outcome-focused. Calm authority. "We lower high-cost medical claims, reduce COBRA liability, and protect your brand."

Blog, thought leadership

Informed, candid, slightly provocative. Expert at a conference. "Your COBRA subsidy is costing you double. Here's the fix."

Blog, founder stories

Personal, direct, unpretentious. Neighbor telling you a story. "Andy was suddenly on COBRA. His family's insurance went up 700%."

Sales outreach

Brief, curious, low-pressure. Casual hallway intro. "Quick question, how are you handling COBRA claims exposure on your self-funded book?"

Customer success / employee-facing

Reassuring, simple, human. A trusted friend who knows insurance. "We're here to help you find coverage that fits your needs and your budget. No jargon, no pressure."

Press / PR

Mission-driven, data-backed, measured. Credible industry voice. "60M+ workforce transitions happen annually. We're building the infrastructure to make each one better."
